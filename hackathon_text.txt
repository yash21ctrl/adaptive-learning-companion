The Big Picture — What Are We Building?CALM is a web-based adaptive learning app that helps students practice Math, Science, and English — while quietly detecting frustration and adjusting in real time to support ADHD and Dyscalculia learners specifically.🎯 Core Rule: The app must feel like a normal, well-designed learning app. It should NEVER feel like a 'disability' tool. The support layer works invisibly in the background — the student never sees a label, only a smoother, kinder experience.The One-Sentence Pitch"An adaptive learning app with a trained AI that detects a student's frustration from HOW they answer — not WHAT they answer — and quietly adjusts difficulty, offers visual help, and knows exactly when to pull them back from the edge of giving up."2. Meet Kip — The AI CharacterKip is a small, friendly mascot that lives in the corner of the Question Screen throughout the whole app. Kip is the emotional face of the AI — students bond with Kip, judges see Kip 'think out loud.'💬 Kip says: "Hey! I'm Kip. I'm not just decoration — I'm always quietly watching how you're doing, so I know exactly when to help."😌 Calm faceLow frustration — Kip is relaxed, just cheering the student on quietly🤔 Curious/concerned faceMedium frustration — Kip leans in, a little more attentive😣 Tired/sad faceHigh frustration — Kip looks like it's noticed something's wrong, right before stepping in🎉 Celebrating faceCorrect streaks — Kip does a little happy bounce animationTap Kip (demo mode only)Expands Kip's real reasoning in plain English — this is what we show judges to prove the AI is genuinely thinking, not just animated3. System ArchitectureFour main pieces, talking to each other:🖥️ Frontend (React)Everything the student sees and clicks: lessons, questions, Kip, results screen. Tracks behavior silently (time, retries, tab-switches, mouse idle, typing pauses) and sends it to the backend.⚙️ Backend (Flask)The traffic controller. Receives answers + behavior data, decides what happens next, talks to the AI layer, saves everything to the database.🧠 AI Layer (3 parts now!)Part 1 — trained Decision Tree classifier predicts frustration. Part 2 — a verification pass double-checks the prediction against the raw numbers. Part 3 — an LLM turns the final, verified prediction into Kip's natural-language reasoning.🗄️ DatabaseStores all questions (tagged by subject/topic/sub-topic/difficulty) and every session's tracked data + results — used to run the app AND to train the AI.🔍 NEW: The Verification Layer (double-checking Kip's instincts)We don't just trust the trained classifier blindly. Before Kip acts on a prediction, a second pass checks: does this prediction actually make sense given the raw numbers?Example: classifier predicts 'High frustration' — but the raw numbers show 0 retries, 4 seconds taken, no tab switches. That's a contradiction. The verification layer catches this and corrects it to 'Low' before Kip reacts.This verification step is done by sending the classifier's prediction + the raw behavior numbers to an LLM, asking it to sanity-check whether the prediction logically matches the dataIt catches OBVIOUS mismatches (a genuine safety net for weird individual predictions) — it does NOT fix a systematically biased training dataset, that's a separate concern handled during data collectionThis is a real, legitimate 'AI reviewing AI' technique, and a strong technical talking point: 'our system doesn't blindly trust its own model — it double-checks itself before acting'How data flows (in order)1. Student answers a question in the Frontend2. Frontend sends the answer + behavior data to the Backend3. Backend checks correctness, saves the record to the Database4. Backend sends behavior data to the trained Classifier → gets a frustration prediction5. The Verification pass checks: does this prediction make sense given the raw numbers? Corrects it if not6. Backend decides the next question (same topic, adjusted difficulty) based on the VERIFIED prediction7. If High frustration is confirmed → trigger a Kip Reset Mission (Section 6) before the easier question appears8. (Demo-only) The final verified prediction is sent to the LLM to generate Kip's plain-English reasoning, shown only when Kip is tapped in demo mode4. Kip's Reset Missions — NEW FEATUREWhen High frustration is confirmed (after verification), Kip doesn't just drop the difficulty quietly. Kip steps in FIRST with a quick, playful reset — a genuine mental breather before the easier question appears.💬 Kip says: "Whoa, hang on — let's shake things up for 10 seconds before we continue!"The Mission Pool (Kip picks one at random each time — never predictable!)🍕 Silly personal question'Quick — what's your favorite snack right now?' / 'Who's your superhero?' — no right or wrong answer, purely a mental palette-cleanser🤸 Physical reset'Give me 3 jumping jacks!' / 'Touch your toes and count to 5!' — gets the body moving for a few seconds🎮 Micro-game (stretch goal)A tiny 10-second reaction game (tap the circle when it turns green) or a quick round of rock-paper-scissors against Kip🌈 Imagination prompt'If you were a superhero right now, what's your power?' — playful, zero pressure🌬️ Breathing beatKip does a little animated breathing bounce, student just watches/follows for 10 seconds — calm, wordless resetThe Full Sequence1. High frustration confirmed by the verification layer2. Kip appears prominently on screen (not just the small corner icon — takes over briefly)3. Kip presents ONE randomly picked Reset Mission from the pool above4. Student completes the quick mission (answers the silly question, does the movement, plays the mini-game, or just follows the breathing beat)5. Kip reacts warmly — 'Nice! Feeling refreshed?' — small celebratory animation6. THEN the easier question (same sub-topic, lower difficulty) appears, continuing the session🎨 Build priority: text-based missions (silly questions + physical prompts) are cheap and high-charm — build these FIRST. The micro-game and breathing animation are stretch goals, add only if time allows after the core app works.7.
